Analysis of pH Levels in Global Water Quality

Why pH Was Chosen as an Indicator

pH is a critical water quality parameter that indicates the acidity or alkalinity of water. It influences chemical and biological processes in aquatic environments and affects the solubility and toxicity of pollutants. A pH range of 6.5 to 8.5 is generally considered acceptable for healthy ecosystems. Extreme pH levels can harm aquatic organisms and compromise water usability for drinking, irrigation, and industry.

Number of pH Measurements per Country

India and Mexico contributed the most pH observations. This may reflect monitoring capacity, population size, or national priorities in water quality.

Average pH by Country

Most countries fall within a safe pH range. However, countries like the Netherlands report significantly high average values (>14), which may indicate industrial influences or data inaccuracies. Denmark and Norway showed slightly lower averages, potentially reflecting acidification concerns.

pH Trends Over Time

  • Poland: Gradual increase from ~8 to 8.8, indicating improved neutrality
  • Netherlands & Norway: Recovery from acidic levels in the 1970s to stable ~6.8–7.2
  • Denmark: Decline between 2010–2015 suggests deterioration

Geographical Map of Average pH

The map shows spatial variation, with Western Europe showing moderate pH levels, while some South Asian and South American countries trend higher. This helps highlight regions where environmental monitoring and policy attention may be needed.

Top 10 Countries with Highest pH
