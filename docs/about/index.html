Welcome to AquaLens

This platform was crafted by the AquaLens team in collaboration with the City of Amsterdam. AquaLens is a project team composed of final-year students from the University of Amsterdam's BSc in Computational Social Science.

Our Foundation

📚 Research-Based 🏙️ Urban Innovation

AquaLens extends research from "Integral Design Method Public Space", a publication exploring Amsterdam's urban transformation challenges in the face of climate change, energy transitions, and resource scarcity.

🏗️ Traditional top-down city planning is obsolete. Underground-first design creates space for water management, energy systems, and climate resilience.

The book introduces integral programming methods where public and private parties collaborate early to reserve subsurface space for essential systems while creating attractive public spaces above ground.

Water quality isn't separate from urban planning; it's central to it. The Dutch case studies you'll explore demonstrate how this research translates into real-world community innovations.

De Ceuvel

🌱 Circular Community ♻️ Zero Waste

Imagine a fully circular community built on a former shipyard in Amsterdam's north. De Ceuvel isn't just sustainable, it's a living laboratory where every drop of water tells a story.

🚿 Revolutionary water systems: Helophyte filters + rainwater harvesting + struvite reactors = closed-loop perfection

Helophyte filters naturally purify wastewater. Community pumps deliver crystal-clear drinking water from captured rainwater. Struvite reactors extract valuable nutrients from human waste, creating fertile soil.

The café's produce grows in an aquaponic greenhouse where fish waste feeds plants, plants filter water, and fish thrive again. But here's the genius: compost toilets replaced sewers entirely due to soil pollution, turning constraint into innovation. Find out more here.

💡 Breaking convention leads to better outcomes. Water quality doesn't limit function, it enhances it.

Lake Lowlands

🎵 Festival Power 🚴 Filtration Bikes

The Netherlands hosts 1,100+ festivals annually, one of the world's highest festival densities. Each event typically means water pollution. Lowlands Festival flipped the script.

🏆 2024 Game-Changer: Festival-goers became water warriors, competing to clean "Lake Lowlands" with custom filtration bikes.

Instead of resigned acceptance of pollution, preserving the lake became interactive entertainment. Festival-goers competed and collaborated, pedaling filtration bikes to maintain water clarity throughout the event.

This breakthrough contributed to critical research on smaller water bodies, often overlooked but equally important. The message is clear: water quality management integrates seamlessly with recreation, not against it. Find out more here.

🌊 When communities engage with water protection, both the water and the community thrive.

De Grachtwacht

🛶 Canal Heroes 🗓️ Weekly Impact

Every Sunday, volunteers unite to canoe through Leiden's historic canals, armed with nets and unshakeable determination. Anyone can join, from canal banks or in canoes.

🔍 They don't just clean; they investigate. Tracking plastic sources + studying wildlife impact = systematic change.

Research drives action. De Grachtwacht traces pollution to its origins, making prevention possible. Their most fascinating finds are preserved for ongoing research, building knowledge that protects wildlife.

But here's the magic: clean water becomes community building. De Grachtwacht creates civic pride that can't be replicated any other way. Protecting water connects people. Find out more here.

💪 Clean water isn't divisive; it's the ultimate unifying force.
